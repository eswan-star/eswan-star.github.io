About

I grew up in rural Eastern Oregon and came to data science through farming. As a vegetable farmer, I saw how much a single decision about water, timing, or crop choice can matter, and how little good information farmers often have when they make it. That led me to a B.S. in Mathematical Biology and Botany at Oregon State University and a Master of Engineering in Applied Computation at Harvard.

Today I use satellite data, USDA agricultural data, and machine learning to monitor crop yields and irrigation resources in the rural Western United States, and I teach AI and data science to students and communities who are too often left out of the technology conversation.

I'm also drawn to the frontier questions where plants, light, and computing meet: growing food in space, how photosynthesis works and what it can teach us, and the quantum and AI hardware that will power the next generation of computing.

Research

Satellite and machine learning monitoring of irrigation in Eastern Oregon

Master's thesis, Harvard University, 2024 · Committee: Pavlos Protopapas, Todd Zickler, Peter Huybers

I built satellite-data and time-series pipelines that combine USDA NASS tabular and satellite data with machine learning to monitor agricultural yields, and used NSIDC data to assess water resources for irrigation. The goal is to give farmers and policymakers the information they need for sound agriculture and water management decisions in the rural West.

Earth observation and food security

Faculty mentor, NASA Space Grant STARR Scholarship, Eastern Oregon University, 2025–2026

I mentored student researcher Brooke Glaser on a review of how Landsat data supports agricultural decision-making, covering NDVI crop monitoring, U.S. data-access policy, NASA Harvest, NASA Acres, and FEWS NET, with a case study of drought monitoring in Uganda's Karamoja region.

Projects & Code

HDSI Agri Datathon

Technical Lead, Harvard Data Science Initiative, 2024

Harvard Data Science Initiative's first-ever datathon, held October 4–6, 2024 with USDA NASS, brought together 20 student teams from across Greater Boston. I designed the technical challenges with USDA NASS, on satellite-based crop phenology and NDVI forecasting, farmer demographics, and crop diversification, and brought federal agricultural data into Google Cloud and Colab for the teams. The idea came from a USDA Agricultural Census session at the 2023 Agricultural Outlook Forum, and from wanting to bring USDA data to universities that rarely work with it.

AI + AG and "See Like a Satellite"

🏆 First place, 14th annual Oregon Ag Fest Agricultural Education Awards, 2026

I designed and teach GO STEM's Artificial Intelligence and Agriculture (AI + AG) program, which shows rural students how AI and satellite data can support crop monitoring and water management. It has reached nearly 500 middle and high school students and more than 70 agriculture and CTE teachers across seven Eastern Oregon counties. Its hands-on labs, including "See Like a Satellite," connect Landsat observations to real decisions on the farm.
